# Narration script — "The problem with most messaging apps" (intro)

No audio yet. Source of truth for the transcript and the `.vtt`. Total ≈ 1:45.

**[00:00–00:22]** Think about how you message someone on most apps today. Your
message goes from your phone to the company's servers, gets stored there, and is
then sent on to your friend. The key detail is that middle step: the company
keeps a readable copy of what you wrote.

**[00:22–00:45]** That copy is useful. It powers search, backups, and syncing
across your devices. But it also means your message is only as private as the
company's promise not to look, and the security of everything that promise relies
on.

**[00:45–01:15]** That readable copy can be used to target ads or train models,
because what you say and who you say it to is valuable. It can be handed to a
government or law enforcement on demand, including for reasons that have nothing
to do with any crime. And it can be stolen in a breach, because a central
readable copy is a tempting target.

**[01:15–01:45]** None of this needs the company to be evil. A good company can
still be bought, breached, or ordered by a court, and a policy can change. The
only real fix is to build the app so the company cannot read your messages at
all. That is what Pollis does, and the rest of this section explains how.
