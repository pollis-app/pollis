Subprocessors

Every third party that touches Pollis, what each one sees, and where it runs.

No third party on this page can read a message. Message content is end-to-end encrypted under MLS and the keys never leave your devices. That is enforced by cryptography, not policy, and holds for every row below.

What they do see is metadata, and that varies a lot. One sees your email address and one-time login code in plain text; others see when you were active. The table says which is which.

Where this list ends

There is no analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting or advertising subprocessor, and no payment processor. No such SDK is in the codebase.

Providers' own operational data is governed by their policies. That includes Cloudflare edge logs, Turso platform logs, the media server's telemetry and APNs/FCM delivery records. This page says what each party can observe; it cannot promise what they keep.

Recipients are a limit too. Delivered messages live in recipients' local encrypted databases, which Pollis cannot reach.

The relay pool, and who operates it

Pollis has an optional relay overlay. When the app connects through a relay, the first-party services do not see your IP address.

It is opt-in and off by default. Unless you have turned on network privacy in preferences, none of your traffic goes through a relay and this section does not apply to you. With network privacy set to prefer, the app goes through a relay when the signed directory lists a reachable one and connects directly otherwise. With strict, sending pauses rather than connecting directly; switch to prefer to keep sending.

How the pool is configured
Operators Every node is first-party, in one AWS account.
Jurisdiction Four US regions (Virginia, Ohio, California, Oregon). All nodes are in the United States.
Placement Each node's region is redrawn at random every 24 hours, with replacement, so two nodes can share a region. There is no stable per-region roster.
Size At most 3 nodes, on the cheapest ARM instances AWS sells.
Control plane In us-west-2. It sees every relay's IP.

The overlay hides your network address, not your account. With multiple hops no single node holds both your IP and your destination, and every hop verifies your device certificate, so it learns which account built the circuit. Push notifications reach your device through APNs and FCM directly; see the Expo row above.
